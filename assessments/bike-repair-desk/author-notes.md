# Bike Repair Desk source notes

Bicycle repairs give this assessment a reason to distinguish a price estimate,
a reservation, and the later use of a part. A mechanic can quote labor and
materials before the shop sets parts aside. Staff can move the work order to
another location, where the job needs a new stock reservation.
[Lightspeed Retail work order guide](https://retail-support.lightspeedhq.com/hc/en-us/articles/229131428-Creating-and-completing-work-orders)

The two shops, job numbers, prices, and examples are fictional. This exercise
uses one simplified shop policy. Customer approval requires all quoted parts
to be available. An approved move requires all parts at the destination.
Completion consumes the parts and returns the amount quoted. The exercise
focuses on quotes, part reservations, and physical stock.

Each visible case follows a job a counter worker can recognize, such as two
customers needing the same chain or a second location with only half the
required stock. The hidden cases check failed approvals and transfers, repeat
closure, and the difference between stocked and available units.
